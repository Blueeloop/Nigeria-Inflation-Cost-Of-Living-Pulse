# Concepts glossary

A plain-English log of concepts as they arise during the analysis. Filled in gradually.

| Concept | Where it comes up | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CPI index vs inflation rate | Dataset inspection | The CPI index is a price level relative to a base year. The inflation rate is the percentage change in that index over time. This project compares rates, not raw index levels across different CPI bases. |
| MoM vs YoY inflation | Trend analysis | MoM compares this month with the previous month and shows near-term price pressure. YoY compares this month with the same month a year earlier and is the main measure used for the long-run trend. |
| Headline, food and core inflation | Trend analysis | Headline is the all-items inflation rate. Food tracks the NBS food index. Core tracks NBS all items less farm produce and energy. Together they show whether pressure is broad-based or concentrated in food. |
| Inflation rate vs contribution to inflation | Category analysis | A category's inflation rate says how fast its own prices rose. Its contribution to headline inflation also reflects its basket weight, so a large category with a moderate rate can add more than a small category with a high rate. |
| CPI weights | Category analysis | Weights are the basket shares used to combine category rates into an overall measure. They are specific to each CPI base and changed when the CPI was rebased in 2024. |
| Inflation slowing vs prices falling | Interpretation | Slower inflation means prices are still rising, but at a reduced rate. Prices fall only when the inflation rate is negative, which is deflation. |
| Descriptive statistics | Statistical analysis | Summaries such as averages, minimums, maximums and ranges describe what happened in the data. They are useful for summarising patterns but do not prove why those patterns occurred. |
| Volatility | Statistical analysis | Volatility describes how much a series moves up and down. A more volatile inflation series changes more sharply from month to month or year to year. |
| Correlation vs causation | Interpretation | Correlation means two series move together. Causation means one directly produces the other. This project describes patterns and possible context without claiming causal effects. |
| Structural breaks and CPI rebasing | Trend analysis | A structural break is a change in how a series is measured. The 2024 CPI rebasing changed the base year, basket and weights, so the analysis keeps the 2009 and 2024 bases separate. |
